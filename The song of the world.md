In the beginning there were 13.

The 13 original Nyame. Their first great act was not creation but agreement. They reached toward each other and joined their power, the way rivers join, not losing themselves but becoming something larger than either could be alone.

By combining their power and technique they flowed. Commanding and moving the ravaging Makulu. They sang the land into rising. They sang the sky into lifting away from the earth like a held breath finally released. They sang the oceans into their basins, the forests into their communities, the creatures into their forms.

For many generations the knowledge about this technique moved from mouth to ear, hand to watching eye, the living passing it to the living. Every griot passing it on carefully and completely. Fragile but alive.

Then the scholars came.

They were not wrong to write it down. There was too much. Memory is faithful but it is not infinite, and the techniques were becoming distorted in the passing, fraying at the edges like old cloth.

So they wrote it down. All of it, or as much as survived. The techniques, the phrases, the ways of listening, the ways of moving the Makulu through the Song.

They called it _the Song of the World._

And it was, as far as anyone knows, the most dangerous collection of knowledge ever set to paper.

With all things powerful, it corrupts. Different people desired this power for themselves. One mortal could not keep his temptation suppressed. The Songstealer as he has come to be known, found it.

How? does it matter?

What matters is that the Songstealer took the texts and ran.

Some say the Songstealer tried to use what was written, to command the Makulu alone, the way a person might try to dam a river with their bare hands. Others say he could not steal the full song, only a few pages and so his triumph was not complete. Some say the Nyame lashed out in anger. That the Songstealer was hunted across the face of the world and driven into hiding before the texts could be used at all.

The griots do not know which is true. They say so plainly.

What they agree on is the ending, because the ending is simply a fact:

The texts were hidden. And they have not been found.

The strange thing is that some people hear it.

Not the texts themselves. Not words on a page. But something underneath the world, a resonance, a pull in a particular direction. The griots say this is the Song itself. That knowledge written down does not simply wait quietly. That it _calls._ That somewhere the texts are still singing. 

Whether this is true is not something for a griot to prove.
But there are those who have heard it.

Perhaps you have heard it too.
